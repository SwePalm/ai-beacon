# Beacon pulse — 2026-10-02

## Outcome

`watch`

An empirical EU account closes part of the previously recorded gap: it observes
real chatbot-to-person handovers in two Dutch service organisations. It makes
the customer-side interaction after a handover visible, but not the worker's
authority, workload, or outcome beyond conversational progress. This strengthens
the narrow handoff-and-recovery watch; it does not establish an agent-work
condition, a cross-centre shift, or grounds for a full run.

## Due reviews and open claims

No formal lifecycle review is due; readiness hypotheses remain scheduled for
2026-11-16. This EU study offers a bounded empirical comparison for
`rh-2026-08-006` and `rh-2026-08-007`, but does not corroborate them: it does
not show the receiving worker's time, authority, or consequences. No calibration
heuristic was due or added.

## Collection gaps

- The new EU trace is strong on conversation but weak on work: it does not show
  the human agent's capacity, discretion, accountability, or resolution result.
- China is still represented by an official standard and vendor interpretations,
  rather than an attributable implementation or affected-person account.
- The US still lacks a non-vendor workflow account of the whole transition.
- Current evidence remains customer-contact heavy and should not be silently
  generalised to persistent-agent or knowledge-work collaboration.

## Next attention

Seek a paired trace—preferably in the US or EU—that joins the customer or other
affected person's transition to the receiving worker's perspective: task,
handoff trigger, inherited context, authority and time, then recovery or
downstream consequence. Treat the Dutch study as a bounded comparator, not
evidence that a particular handoff script should be prescribed.
