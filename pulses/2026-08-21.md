# Beacon pulse — 2026-08-21

## Outcome

`material change`

The material change is institutional, not a claim of broad behaviour change:
on 2 August the EU AI Act's transparency obligations and enforcement arrangements
entered into application. The US and China observations below show continuing
provider-led expansion of agentic work infrastructure, but do not establish
ordinary adoption, benefit, or harm.

## Due reviews and open claims

No formal signal lifecycle review is due: the existing readiness hypotheses are
scheduled for 2026-11-16. The persistent-agent-work run's open claims remain
unresolved. This pulse strengthens the case for collecting workflow traces and
participant accounts; it does not corroborate those claims.

No calibration heuristic was due or added.

## Collection gaps

- All three retained items are institutional or provider sources. There is no
  consented account from a person who commissions, supervises, or is affected by
  an agentic workflow.
- The three-centre lens is present, but source types are not equivalent: EU is
  a public authority; US and China are suppliers describing their own systems.
- No evidence yet connects EU transparency enforcement to an everyday workflow,
  and no independent China source tests Alibaba's claimed infrastructure in use.
- The pulse was English-language and has no occupational, small-organisation,
  labour, or public-service perspective.

## Next attention

For the next pulse, seek one independent, attributable account of a
non-technical workflow using persistent or delegated AI work, ideally one that
shows who sets boundaries, reviews exceptions, and carries responsibility. Also
watch for the first observable implementation effects of EU transparency rules.
