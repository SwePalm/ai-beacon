# Beacon: Founding Handover

## Why this exists

AI is changing more than the speed or cost of particular tasks. It is changing expectations, roles, choices, and the conditions under which people participate in work and life. Many people want to participate but do not want—or have the time—to become AI specialists.

Beacon is a long-lived instrument for making those changes legible. It observes patterns, compares them with history, and turns them into non-prescriptive Choice Briefs.

## The central promise

Beacon does not tell readers what future to choose. It helps them notice the choice terrain before defaults harden around them.

The project is principled about method: show receipts, name uncertainty, separate evidence from interpretation, include counter-cases, and disclose values. It is deliberately non-prescriptive about conclusions.

## Audience

The primary audience is the middle 60%: capable, busy people who want a meaningful part in the transition but are neither building frontier systems nor opting out of change. Beacon must translate without patronising and never confuse elite practice with ordinary conditions.

## The master artifact

Every cycle creates one **Beacon Choice Brief**:

1. **The condition** — what appears to be changing.
2. **The evidence terrain** — what is observed, reported, researched, or merely signalled.
3. **The historical rhyme** — where a similar shift has happened and where the analogy breaks.
4. **The human question** — what may now matter for people, teams, or institutions.
5. **The choice terrain** — what could be cultivated, protected, redesigned, or contested.
6. **Counter-case and uncertainty** — what could make the interpretation incomplete or wrong.
7. **Scenario resonance** — how the finding bears on relevant Attunement scenarios.
8. **Open claims** — what later cycles must check.

## Method invariants

- A source is not a claim, and a signal is not evidence.
- Engagement is a discovery clue, never proof of prevalence or importance.
- No single anecdote represents the middle 60%.
- Historical analogy is a challenge to the present story, not decorative context.
- A readiness hypothesis may recommend learning, protection, redesign, or deliberate non-adoption; it never assumes the answer is individual upskilling.
- The pipeline may propose changes to itself, but only a human can approve a method change.
- Every important assertion must retain a dated source and an evidence status.

## Initial delivery sequence

1. Complete and validate the portable skill contracts.
2. Define initial ledgers and run schemas.
3. Create a small historical baseline using three to five comparable past shifts.
4. Run one narrow pilot condition.
5. Review the first Choice Brief for usefulness with ordinary working people through conversation, not a form.
6. Amend the instrument only after the pilot leaves evidence of what failed or was missing.

## Deferred decisions

- Final project and artifact names.
- Public format and publishing cadence.
- MCP App or other consumption surface.
- Full source integrations and automation depth.
