# Historical rhyme — background systems, not generic automation

## Comparator: workflow systems that persist between human interventions

Earlier workflow software, schedulers, and managed services moved some work from a person repeatedly initiating each step to systems that continued in the background. The comparison is useful because it asks who monitors a process, handles exceptions, and is accountable when a system crosses organisational boundaries.

**Similarity.** Persistent agents also introduce continuity, triggers, queues, access rights, logs, and exceptions. They can create a new role for people who commission, monitor, and intervene rather than carry every step directly.

**Difference.** Agentic systems can interpret natural-language goals, select tools, and change their path during execution. Their scope is less fixed than a conventional workflow, so a familiar automation control may not be enough.

**Affected groups visible here.** Operators, administrators, and task owners are visible. People whose data, messages, accounts, or opportunities are affected indirectly are easier to miss.

**Evidence limit.** This is a conceptual comparator, not a causal historical forecast. The baseline pilot contains evidence about task redistribution but does not establish the effects of persistence or autonomous tool selection.

**Lesson not to import.** Do not call agents “just workflow automation,” or assume that a dashboard, log, or approval button automatically creates genuine oversight.
