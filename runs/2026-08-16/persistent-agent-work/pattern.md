# Pattern synthesis — provisional

## Bounded interpretation

**Interpretation:** A shift from discrete AI exchanges toward delegated, persistent agent work may redistribute the work of commissioning, boundary-setting, monitoring, exception handling, integration, and accountable validation. Whether this is experienced as expanded agency, hidden burden, or both may depend on access, permissions, workload design, and recourse.

The evidence supports product availability, reported provider usage patterns, and a small reported use case. It does not establish broad adoption, social value, or a settled capability requirement.

## Cross-centre terrain

- **US:** provider research and product controls show longer-horizon work and oversight artefacts, within provider-specific samples and systems.
- **EU:** a provider's durable-workflow product foregrounds observability, retries, and human approval for production processes.
- **China:** provider and reported-use signals show agentic systems embedded in collaboration platforms and individual use settings; distribution and effect remain unproven.

The centres are not interchangeable, and this record does not claim a three-way prevalence comparison.

## Capability terrain, not a skill prescription

- A person may need room to frame intent and validate a resulting artifact, but that responsibility should not be silently shifted onto individuals.
- A team may need shared practices for context, access boundaries, escalation, and handoff.
- An institution may need permissions, logs, approval design, cost control, and a way to repair errors.
- Public systems may need to consider access, accountability, and whether uneven agent capacity reshapes participation.

## Counter-case

If agents remain unreliable, too costly, tightly controlled, or useful only in specialist settings, the observed product movement may not become an ordinary-work condition. Conversely, a successful agent can still add checking and coordination work rather than remove it.
