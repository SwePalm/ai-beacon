# Selection — persistent agent work

## Condition

**People and organisations are beginning to delegate multi-step work to AI agents that can persist across tools, files, sessions, and time, rather than using AI only through discrete exchanges.**

November 2025 is a useful observation point, not a claim that a single model threshold caused the movement. The run follows the condition through product artefacts, usage research, and early reports across the US, EU, and China.

## Linked themes

`work`, `agency`, `judgment`, `learning`, `trust`, `power`, and `care`.

## Why this may matter beyond frontier builders

Delegated work changes more than task speed. It can change who frames a task, grants access, monitors exceptions, validates an output, carries responsibility, and can decline or interrupt the work. These questions apply outside coding, including research, operations, administration, communications, and personal coordination.

## Initial question

When AI moves from answering prompts to carrying delegated work across time and tools, what human, team, institutional, and public capabilities become more visible, redistributed, or newly contested?

## Counter-interpretations

- The shift may remain concentrated among unusually resourced users or organisations.
- Product capability claims, activity logs, and adoption claims do not establish value, safety, or broad use.
- More delegation may coexist with more collaboration; it is not a one-way replacement of human work.
- Persistent operation may require more, rather than less, human coordination and accountability.

## Exclusions

- No claim that agents replace occupations or that people must run agents continuously.
- No inference that a dedicated computer, agent fleet, or high usage is typical.
- No ranking of products or models.
- No presumption that a capability requirement belongs to an individual rather than a team, employer, provider, or public institution.
