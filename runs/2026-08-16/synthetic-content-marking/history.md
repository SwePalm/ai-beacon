# Historical rhyme — labels, metadata, and provenance

## Comparator: earlier provenance practices in digital media

Digital media has long carried provenance-related information through metadata, digital signatures, and watermarks. NIST's technical overview describes these as different mechanisms rather than a single proof system ([NIST](https://www.nist.gov/publications/reducing-risks-posed-synthetic-content-overview-technical-approaches-digital-content)).

**Similarity.** As with earlier metadata and authenticity systems, a marker can make a claim about a file's origin or history available for a later tool or recipient to inspect.

**Difference.** Generative AI marking may be applied at generation, interface, file, platform, and distribution stages; the social question includes disclosure of assistance and human responsibility, not merely a file's technical history.

**Affected groups visible in this comparison.** Technical providers and verifiers are visible. People who cannot inspect markers, people wrongly treated as deceptive, and people working through copied, edited, or inaccessible formats are not adequately represented.

**Evidence limits.** This is a technical and institutional comparison, not a history of user experience or enforcement.

**Lesson not to import.** Do not assume that because a provenance mechanism is machine-readable, it is legible, fair, durable, or decisive for a person facing a judgement about their work.
