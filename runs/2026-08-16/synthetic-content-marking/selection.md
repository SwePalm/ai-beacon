# Selection — synthetic-content marking across development centres

## Live condition

**Machine-readable and visible markers for AI-generated content are becoming part of the infrastructure through which people create, publish, assess, and contest digital material.**

This condition was selected because a technical choice, a product response, and regulatory requirements are active across the US, EU, and China. It is narrower than “AI regulation” and affects ordinary work: drafting, editing, education, publishing, customer communication, and verification.

## Linked themes

`trust`, `judgment`, `identity`, `creation`, `work`, `power`, and `public`.

## Initial question

When content may carry a visible or machine-readable AI marker, what new verification, disclosure, error, and recourse conditions arise for people who make, edit, publish, receive, or are represented by it?

## Cross-centre frame

| Centre | Signal to investigate | What must not be assumed |
|---|---|---|
| US | Provider response and voluntary technical standards/research. | That a voluntary technical approach is universal, effective, or law. |
| EU | Article 50 transparency obligations and implementation guidance. | That an EU obligation is technically simple or automatically global. |
| China | Binding labelling measures and national standard. | That similar wording produces the same governance purpose, practice, or recourse. |

## Counter-interpretations to retain

- A marker may assist provenance without proving factual accuracy, authorship, intent, or harmfulness.
- Marks can be absent because content is human-made, edited, outside scope, technically unmarked, or because metadata did not travel; absence is not proof.
- Disclosure may shift responsibility and workload onto individual creators, editors, or recipients rather than providers.
- Global product behaviour can be influenced by law in one place, but it may also reflect provider choice, standards, or product strategy.

## Exclusions

- No legal-compliance determination for any organisation or person.
- No claim that one system is better, more democratic, or more effective.
- No claim that watermarks reliably detect all AI content.
- No instruction to disclose, label, monitor, or sanction a particular person.
